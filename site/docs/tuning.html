Varispeed and key alignment

Varispeed changes pitch and duration together, like tape speed. A shift of s semitones multiplies playback speed by 2^(s/12) and divides duration by that factor. Saved kits retain transformed audio and metadata recording the cumulative shift, including changes made before key alignment.

Choose semitones

Desktop App → 01 / Samples: select one or more kit rows and use Lower pitch… (J) or Raise pitch… (K). Shortcuts work while the sample list has focus. Ctrl/Shift selection applies the entered value to several samples together.

The direction sets the sign; enter only 0–99. Zero leaves audio unchanged. A second operation adds to the total displayed beside the sample. J + 07 marks −7, speed ×0.6674, duration ×1.4983; K + 24 marks +24, speed ×4, duration ÷4. The kit meter measures the transformed audio. A single sample over 65 seconds is refused; a saved kit must fit within 100 slots and 65 seconds.

Align a completed kit

Open Align kit keys… in the Desktop App. Alignment is optional and works offline. The Desktop App does nothing until you ask: choose the master, tick the samples to align and press Align selected. Only the master and the ticked samples are listened to and transposed; rows already selected in Samples come ticked.

  1. Choose a reference sample in Master key, then tick the other samples to transpose. The reference sample stays unchanged. All slaves and No slaves help with large kits.
  2. Press Align selected. Filename notes are compared with measured audio notes; both include prior varispeed. When they disagree, explicitly choose Filename or Measured for that sample and press Align selected again. Nothing is rendered until relevant discrepancies are resolved.
  3. Review additional shifts, cumulative semitones and the resulting duration. Automatic shifts match the reference sample’s pitch class, using the nearest transposition within −6…+5 semitones. This keeps the existing register as far as the nearest choice allows. It does not convert major/minor modes or reharmonize chords and phrases.
  4. Edit individual shifts or apply one manual amount to several selected samples together, then press Align selected again. Manual amounts explicitly bypass pitch inference for those samples. You can return to automatic calculation. Over-budget results are not rendered until adjusted.
  5. Accept aligned kit replaces the draft only with complete outputs whose measured total fits. Cancel leaves it intact. Save normally afterwards; transfer remains a separate confirmation.

Samples with neither a clear filename note nor a reliable monophonic estimate are listed as excluded and kept unchanged. They remain available for manual varispeed. An excluded sample does not block the others; an unclear reference sample does. The estimator is not musical-key or chord detection. Drum transients, polyphony and changing melodies may be uncertain even when a filename contains a note.

Filename recognition accepts distinct tokens such as F#3, Bb, Cmin and D_major. Multiple different note tokens are ambiguous. Wrong filename labels and octave errors in measured pitch remain possible; both sources are visible. Pitch confidence is an estimate, not a guarantee. A ? beside a shift means older transformation history cannot be fully recovered (for example, a missing parent file). Filename-based inference is disabled for that uncertain history; actual audio can still supply a measured note.

Slot order, unselected samples and source libraries remain intact. Completed variants may remain available if a later render fails or is cancelled; they do not enter the draft unless the complete operation succeeds.
